## 6. Design implications and limits

A temporal-access diagnostic starts with the forecast product. Fix the history span, native target endpoints, target mask, loss and source-validation selection rule, and preserve them across fine and coarsened inputs. Paired endpoint MSE measures the sum of squared level and detail errors; paired endpoint MAE measures their maximum absolute error. The exact finite-support example establishes that their optimal access rankings can differ for the same target. A gain in detail reconstruction therefore needs a separate assessment of endpoint utility, as illustrated by the six 2014 cell means: fine histories improve detail MSE while worsening normalized endpoint MAE.

The information comparison begins after the declared access map. Fine raw pairs contain their averages, so an unrestricted predictor can always ignore extra detail. Fine maximum access and coarse level access instead provide the generally nonnested observations \(U=C+D\) and \(C\). Their Bayes-risk reversal describes this scalar restriction. In the independent, sign-symmetric construction, nonlinear future level \(C+\alpha g(D)\) produces a gap depending on conditional covariance and nonlinear-term predictability through \(W_D-2\alpha K-\alpha^2H_g\). The verified examples preserve a crossover or move it beyond \([0,1]\), showing why magnitude dependence alone is insufficient for a retention rule. Real customers have no estimated \(\alpha\) here; the nonlinear and MAE constructions define mathematical boundaries rather than explanations of their observed contrasts.

Three coordinate comparisons make this analysis operational. Maximum versus separate level/magnitude access assesses mixing; magnitude versus signed access assesses orientation; signed versus direct access assesses finite fitting under an invertible coordinate change. Compensating the first affine preserves the signed/direct initial function up to floating-point error. Common learned shapes, batches, seeds, fitting budgets and source-selected checkpoints on identical endpoints improve comparability, while noninvertible operations retain different function classes. Filtering supplies another declared comparison because it changes neighborhood access without generally recovering the sign removed by maximum pooling. These contrasts should be interpreted together with the selected forecaster's basic source competence.

A source-competence gate should precede external interpretation: compare selected forecasts with frozen simple references on identical source targets. The complete-input MLP extension failed this gate with fixed global mean/standard-deviation normalization. One common source-median/MAD repair was then applied to all 60 conditions. Every selected fit still exceeded the frozen daily-reference loss; the six-core mean source NMAE was 0.213858 against 0.115580 for that reference. The extension stopped without external predictions or further repairs. It therefore contributes a documented fitting-feasibility boundary, with external evidence confined to the residual N-HiTS study. Interpreting an access ranking from such a poorly fitted family would confound representation with forecasting failure.

For the competent residual forecasters, both primary customer/week intervals span zero, and all four multiplicity-adjusted fine-access diagnostic intervals span zero in each phase. The full-network advantage remains unresolved. Earlier forecasts and backcasts, the last-value path and per-window normalization offer possible routes around the final-block restriction. Local perturbation checks establish that block's access behavior; attributing the external contrasts to a particular bypass would require an additional intervention. The phases change customers and dates together, and their comparison cannot isolate calendar drift. The reported uncertainty remains conditional on the three fitted seeds and the observed customer/week sampling structure.

The information-bottleneck formulation [32] offers a framework for compression that preserves task-relevant information, whereas spectral-bias research [33] studies frequency-dependent neural learning. These perspectives motivate distinct representation and fitting questions; they supply neither a proof of the present pooling threshold nor a verified future mechanism for the external customers.

Deployment requires a separate accounting of model, codec, metadata, runtime and local state, with an explicit amortization unit. A useful forecast coordinate and a smaller native array establish different benefits, so a retention recommendation also needs complete operational costs.
