Panels b–d are oracle calculations, with no uncertainty bands or intermediate-α neural fits asserted.

Figure 2. Paired endpoint-loss contrasts in the matched N-HiTS experiment. (a) Primary interaction: maximum fine-minus-coarse normalized MAE minus the corresponding average-access difference. (b) Four fine-history access contrasts. Points average three fixed source-selected fits, and lower differences favor the first named condition. Calibration uses 16 reserved customers in July–December 2013 (27 calendar-week blocks); confirmation uses 32 distinct reserved customers in 2014 (53 blocks). The primary bars are the original pointwise 95% percentile intervals. Diagnostic bars are centered fixed-SE maxT 95% simultaneous intervals over the four diagnostics, separately within each phase. All intervals use 5,000 joint paired draws of customers and calendar-week blocks and condition on the three fitted models. They describe customer/time sampling variation, not training-seed uncertainty. No interval excludes zero. Normalized endpoint MAE uses the fixed customer-specific pre-period mean, floored at 1 kW, with common targets, masks and scales across conditions. The signed/direct comparison concerns finite-fit behavior of invertible coordinates rather than raw-information loss.

Figure 3. Endpoint-specific retention diagnostic. Establish source competence and source-validation selection, then hold forecast endpoints and evaluation scales fixed while checking matched access coordinates. If source competence fails, stop before external testing. Maximum versus separated mean/magnitude probes mixing, magnitude versus signed detail probes orientation, and signed versus direct probes finite-fit coordinate sensitivity. Evaluate the stated endpoint loss and report paired customer/time uncertainty with the declared multiplicity scope before making a source-only retention choice. A new selected deployment requires fresh evaluation. Report full end-to-end resource cost separately from predictive loss, including persistent record, metadata, model and runtime bytes, peak RAM, encode/decode time and model amortization. This schematic organizes the verified argument; it is not a validated selector, a universal retention rule, or an estimate of real-data α.
